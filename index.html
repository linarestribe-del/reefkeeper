Weekly treatment limit: treat 5–10 visible aiptasia at a time. Turn off flow briefly, apply paste/injection, wait, then restart flow.
Do not treat the whole tank at once. Large die-off plus already-high nutrients can irritate coral and worsen instability.
Berghia warning: your wrasses make berghia risky. They may eat them before the nudibranchs establish.
Peppermint shrimp: still hit or miss. They are not a reliable primary plan in this tank.
Phase 1 — No New Coral
Now–6 weeks
Unlock condition: no new tissue loss, alk steady near 8.5–9.0, phosphate under 0.30, salinity verified stable. Protect the coral you already have.
Phase 2 — Soft/LPS Test
After stability
First additions: one inexpensive forgiving frag at a time. Duncan, candy cane, mushroom, or another hardy LPS/soft coral. Wait 2–3 weeks between additions.
Phase 3 — SPS Later
3+ months
Only after: phosphate near 0.10–0.15, coralline returning, alk stable for months, and existing LPS fully expanded. Start with montipora or birdsnest, not acropora.
Good signs: Duncan extension, hammer inflation, candy cane tissue fullness, BTAs staying put, fish eating aggressively, and new coralline spots.
Warning signs: BTAs wandering, Duncan closing, hammer tissue recession, wrasses hiding, sudden algae burst, or skimmer production changing sharply.
Immediate checks: any sudden coral decline means test salinity, alkalinity, phosphate, temperature, and verify ATO/RODI function before doing anything else.
