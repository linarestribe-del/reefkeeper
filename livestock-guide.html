Fish

Clownfish pair

Amphiprion ocellaris (verify exact species)

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reefs; your pair hosts the Duncan coral and regularly lays eggs.

  • Territorial pair-bonding damselfish; spawning is a sign of comfort and routine.
  • Can host anemones, but also often adopts corals such as Duncan, hammer, or leather corals.
  • Watch the hosted coral for irritation if the pair becomes too rough.

Your tank: Your tank note: breeding pair; currently using the Duncan as their host.

Fish

Yellow coris / canary wrasse

Halichoeres chrysus

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reef flats and lagoons.

  • Sand-sleeping wrasse; needs a sand bed for normal resting behavior.
  • Active forager that may pick at small pests and benthic invertebrates.
  • Needs a tight lid because wrasses are strong jumpers.

Your tank: Your tank note: active wrasse community member.

Fish

Melanurus wrasse

Halichoeres melanurus

Natural range / location: Western Pacific and Indo-Pacific reef habitats.

  • Useful active hunter in mixed reef systems.
  • Sleeps in sand and may dive when startled.
  • Can outcompete slower feeders, so observe feeding balance.

Your tank: Your tank note: one of the tank’s established wrasses.

Fish

Red Head Solon Fairy Wrasse

Cirrhilabrus solorensis (taxonomy/photos may appear as C. aquamarinus)

Natural range / location: Western Pacific reef slopes and rubble zones.

  • Fairy wrasses are open-water planktivores rather than sand-sleeping Halichoeres wrasses.
  • Color and display behavior are often best with low stress and good nutrition.
  • A tight lid is essential; fairy wrasses jump.

Your tank: Your tank note: blue body, pink/red head, black mark under chin.

Fish

Banggai cardinalfish

Pterapogon kauderni

Natural range / location: Banggai Archipelago, Indonesia; a geographically restricted species.

  • Slow, hovering cardinalfish that appreciates calmer areas.
  • Mouthbrooding species with striking vertical bars and long fins.
  • Does best when not bullied by aggressive feeders.

Your tank: Your tank note: peaceful midwater fish.

Fish

Blue-green chromis

Chromis viridis (verify exact species)

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reefs, often associated with branching corals.

  • Planktivorous damselfish; generally active in the water column.
  • Can be peaceful, but groups may thin over time in aquariums.
  • Benefits from frequent small feedings and good oxygenation.

Your tank: Your tank note: one blue chromis/blue-green chromis present.

Fish

Yellow tang

Zebrasoma flavescens

Natural range / location: Central Pacific, especially Hawaii and nearby reef systems.

  • Constant grazer; provide algae films, nori, and open swimming room.
  • Can become territorial toward similar-shaped tangs.
  • A good long-term indicator of feeding and oxygenation.

Your tank: Your tank note: part of the tang group; watch feeding dominance.

Fish

White-tail bristletooth tang

Ctenochaetus flavicauda

Natural range / location: Central Pacific reef environments.

  • Bristletooth tangs rasp film algae and detritus from rock surfaces.
  • Generally more focused on surfaces than open algae sheets alone.
  • Needs oxygenation, swimming room, and stable water quality.

Your tank: Your tank note: useful film/detritus grazer.

Fish

Desjardini sailfin tang

Zebrasoma desjardinii

Natural range / location: Indian Ocean and Red Sea reef habitats.

  • Large, bold Zebrasoma tang that can outgrow smaller systems.
  • Heavy grazer; contributes substantially to nutrient input through feeding.
  • Needs long-term swimming room and careful tang aggression management.

Your tank: Your tank note: large grazer; account for feeding and bioload.

Fish

Orange-banded / Randall’s prawn goby

Amblyeleotris randalli

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific sand and rubble areas, often living with pistol shrimp.

  • Shrimp-goby relationship: goby watches while the shrimp maintains the burrow.
  • Needs sand/rubble zones and stable rockwork.
  • Can be shy; target feeding may help if food does not reach the burrow.

Your tank: Your tank note: paired with a pistol shrimp.

Pistol shrimp partner

Alpheus spp. (exact species unconfirmed)

Natural range / location: Tropical reef sand/rubble burrows; exact origin depends on species.

  • Maintains the shared burrow with the goby.
  • The “pistol” snap can be heard when it closes its claw rapidly.
  • Requires stable rockwork so burrowing does not undermine structures.

Your tank: Your tank note: paired with the orange-banded goby; add your own photo if possible.

Anemone

Bubble-tip anemone

Entacmaea quadricolor

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reef habitats.

  • Can move or split when stressed or growing.
  • Powerful stinging neighbor; allow space from corals and mushrooms.
  • Stable light, flow, and water quality are more important than constant adjustment.

Your tank: Your tank note: two BTAs, stationary about a year; mushrooms below may be affected.

Coral

Duncan coral

Duncanopsammia axifuga

Natural range / location: Australia and nearby Indo-Pacific reef areas.

  • LPS coral with fleshy polyps that often accepts meaty foods.
  • Usually a forgiving coral, but still reacts to instability and irritation.
  • Can be irritated if clownfish host it too aggressively.

Your tank: Your tank note: healthy and currently hosting the clownfish.

Coral

Hammer coral

Fimbriaphyllia ancora (formerly Euphyllia ancora)

Natural range / location: Indo-West Pacific reef habitats.

  • LPS coral with stinging tentacles; give space from neighbors.
  • Sensitive to alkalinity swings and rapid nutrient changes.
  • Moderate flow should move the polyps without blasting tissue.

Your tank: Your tank note: two surviving colonies; monitor recovery.

Coral

Green candy cane / trumpet coral

Caulastraea furcata

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reef systems.

  • Hardy LPS that can be a useful stability indicator.
  • Accepts target feeding but does not require heavy feeding.
  • Watch tissue fullness and polyp extension as easy visual cues.

Your tank: Your tank note: green candy cane colony present.

Coral

Ricordea mushroom

Ricordea spp. (R. florida/R. yuma should be verified)

Natural range / location: Caribbean for R. florida; Indo-Pacific for R. yuma.

  • Corallimorph mushroom coral; prefers lower to moderate flow.
  • Can be affected by chemical warfare and nearby anemones.
  • Shrinking, bleaching, or detachment are signs to reassess placement and stability.

Your tank: Your tank note: stressed below the BTAs; allelopathy/stinging is a likely concern.

Coral

Grube’s gorgonian

Pinnigorgia flava

Natural range / location: Aquacultured hobby strain originally associated with the Philippines.

  • Photosynthetic soft coral with thin tan to pink branches.
  • Generally regarded as hardy and fast growing in reef aquaria.
  • Benefits from moderate flow to keep branches clean.

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific reef and rubble zones.

  • Bright striped legs and strong cleanup-crew personality.

  • May bother snails or small animals if underfed or shell-limited.

Natural range / location: Indo-Pacific sandy habitats.

  • Lives in and moves through the sand bed.
  • Consumes sandbed fauna and organic matter; can starve in very clean tanks.

Fighting conch

Strombus alatus / Strombus spp. (verify exact species)

Natural range / location: Sandy shallow marine habitats; Florida fighting conch is Caribbean/Florida.

  • Excellent sandbed grazer and detritus processor.
  • Needs open sand area and enough film/detritus to graze.
  • Often visible by its eyes and trunk-like proboscis.
